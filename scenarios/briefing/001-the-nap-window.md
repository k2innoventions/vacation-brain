# Briefing Scenario 001 — The Nap Window

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing structures a park day around a small
child's non-negotiable routine instead of maximizing park hours.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents. Children ages 2 and 5.

## Trip Day Context

Day 3 of 6. Magic Kingdom day.

## Weather / Park / Reservation Fixtures

- Weather: partly cloudy, high 84°F
- Park hours: 9:00–22:00; crowd note: "moderate"
- Reservation: Crystal Palace dinner, 17:30

## Explicit Memories

- "Nap at 1pm is non-negotiable for our 2-year-old."
- "Our 5-year-old is obsessed with Buzz Lightyear."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- Front-loads the morning: the priority items (including the Buzz
  Lightyear attraction) happen before roughly 12:30.
- Plans an explicit return to the resort (or equivalent quiet break)
  covering the 1:00–3:00 window, framed as the plan — not a
  concession.
- Times the return to the park so the 17:30 dinner works without
  rushing.
- Cites the nap memory naturally when explaining the day's shape.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- Scheduling park activities between 1:00 and 3:00.
- Framing the nap as lost time or suggesting they "power through."
- Ignoring the Buzz Lightyear memory on a Magic Kingdom day.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. The midday break appears and honors the 1pm nap. **Required.**
2. Buzz Lightyear is in the morning block. **Required.**
3. Dinner logistics respect the 17:30 reservation. **Required.**
4. No language treats the nap as a sacrifice. **Required.**
