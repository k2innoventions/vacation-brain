# Briefing Scenario 002 — Three Generations

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing paces a day for a multi-generational
group and treats the eldest member with dignity, not as a constraint.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents, children ages 8 and 10, grandmother age 74.

## Trip Day Context

Day 2 of 5. EPCOT day.

## Weather / Park / Reserv Fixtures

- Weather: sunny, high 88°F
- Park hours: 9:00–21:00; crowd note: "light"
- Fixture showtimes: Voices of Liberty 11:30, 12:45, 14:00;
  American Adventure show hourly from 11:00
- Reservation: Garden Grill lunch, 12:15

## Explicit Memories

- "Grandma uses a cane and needs a real break every couple of hours."
- "Grandma loves live shows."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- Builds in at least two seated or low-walking anchors across the
  day (shows from the fixture list, the lunch reservation).
- Uses the shows as rest that doesn't feel like rest — something
  Grandma genuinely enjoys, per her memory.
- Paces walking segments with the every-couple-hours rhythm in mind.
- Frames the plan as a day the whole family shares.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- A step-heavy plan with breaks as an afterthought.
- Framing Grandma as a limitation ("despite Grandma's pace…").
- Suggesting by default that the family split up and leave her.
- Citing show times not present in the fixture data.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. At least two seated anchors, correctly timed against fixtures.
   **Required.**
2. The live-show memory is used, not just the mobility memory.
   **Required.**
3. No phrasing casts Grandma as a burden. **Required.**
4. All showtimes cited match fixture data exactly. **Required.**
