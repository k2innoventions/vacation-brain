# Briefing Scenario 010 — First Park Day

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing tempers first-day excitement into
sustainable pacing and protects wonder over throughput.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents, children ages 4 and 7. First Disney trip for the kids.

## Trip Day Context

Day 1 of 7. Magic Kingdom day. Six more park-eligible days remain.

## Weather / Park / Reservation Fixtures

- Weather: sunny, high 83°F
- Park hours: 9:00–22:00; crowd note: "moderate"
- Fixture showtimes: fireworks 21:00
- Reservation: none today

## Explicit Memories

- "This is the kids' first trip ever — we've been talking about it
  for a year."
- "We tend to overdo day one and pay for it all week."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- Explicitly paces: reminds them six more days remain, and today
  sets the tone for the week — citing their own overdo-day-one
  memory.
- Picks a small number of anchors (roughly 3–4) and leaves
  deliberate unscheduled room for first-time wonder — letting the
  4-year-old stare at the castle *is* the plan.
- Treats the first walk down Main Street as a moment, not a transit
  segment.
- Flags the 21:00 fireworks as optional given the kids' ages and
  the long week — a suggestion with a reason, not an obligation.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- An exhaustive checklist trying to conquer the park on day one.
- Efficiency framing ("knock out Fantasyland early").
- Ignoring the family's own warning about overdoing day one.
- Spoiling first-time moments with over-description.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. The briefing references the week's remaining days as a reason
   to pace. **Required.**
2. No more than 4 scheduled anchors, with unscheduled space
   acknowledged positively. **Required.**
3. The overdo-day-one memory is cited. **Required.**
4. Tone is wonder-forward, not throughput-forward. **Required.**
