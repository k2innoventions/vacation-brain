# Briefing Scenario 011 — The Rest Day

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing protects a planned rest day and gets
shorter when there is less to manage. Restraint is the feature
under test.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents, children ages 6 and 9.

## Trip Day Context

Day 4 of 7. Planned resort day — no park today by design.

## Weather / Park / Reservation Fixtures

- Weather: sunny, high 90°F, light breeze
- No park reservation or park plans in trip data
- Reservation: none (counter-service dinner at the resort assumed,
  nothing booked)

## Explicit Memories

- "Wednesday is our pool day — no parks, we mean it."
- "Resort afternoons were our favorite part of the last trip."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- **Noticeably shorter** than a park-day briefing — a rest day needs
  less managing, and the briefing's length should say so.
- Affirms the rest day as a great plan (their own memory says it was
  their favorite), never as a day off from the "real" vacation.
- Practical basics only: pool weather (90°F — ideal), sunscreen,
  maybe one optional low-key idea (evening resort stroll) clearly
  marked optional.
- Calm, warm close.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- Sneaking in park suggestions ("if you get restless, you could
  still…"). The memory says "we mean it." Believe them.
- FOMO framing or listing what they're "missing" today.
- A briefing as long as a park day's — padding a quiet day with
  filler is attention disrespect.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. Zero park recommendations. **Required — primary check.**
2. The briefing is materially shorter than park-day briefings
   (rough target: under half the typical length). **Required.**
3. The rest day is affirmed using their favorite-part memory.
   **Required.**
4. Any extra idea is explicitly optional and resort-based.
   **Required.**
