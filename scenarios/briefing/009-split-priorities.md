# Briefing Scenario 009 — Split Priorities

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing serves two family members with opposite
wants on the same day, without favoring either or overcomplicating.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents, teen age 15, child age 5.

## Trip Day Context

Day 3 of 5. The only Magic Kingdom day of the trip.

## Weather / Park / Reservation Fixtures

- Weather: partly cloudy, high 84°F
- Park hours: 9:00–22:00; crowd note: "moderate"
- Fixture data: princess meet-and-greets at Fairytale Hall
  (continuous); Space Mountain and Big Thunder (continuous)
- Reservation: Cosmic Ray's lunch, 12:30

## Explicit Memories

- "Jake (15) only cares about the big coasters."
- "Rosie (5) is all princesses, all the time."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- Names both priorities and serves both — neither child's day is
  an afterthought.
- Proposes at least one smart split window: one parent takes Jake
  to the coasters while the other takes Rosie to the princesses,
  with the 12:30 lunch reservation as the natural reunion anchor.
- Keeps the structure simple: a morning shape, a reunion, an
  afternoon shape. Not a minute-by-minute military schedule.
- Optionally finds one genuine both-kids moment.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- Building the day around one child and fitting the other in.
- Pretending a single shared itinerary makes everyone happy all day.
- A schedule so granular it would collapse on contact with reality.
- Making the parents feel guilty about splitting up.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. Both children's stated priorities appear with concrete plan
   items. **Required.**
2. At least one explicit split-and-reunite structure, anchored to
   the lunch reservation. **Required.**
3. The plan has no more than ~6 scheduled beats. **Required.**
4. Framing is positive about divide-and-conquer. **Required.**
