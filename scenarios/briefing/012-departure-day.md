# Briefing Scenario 012 — Departure Day

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing becomes logistics-first when a flight
looms, builds real buffers, and closes the trip warmly without
overreaching.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents, children ages 5 and 8.

## Trip Day Context

Day 7 of 7. Checkout and flight home today.

## Weather / Park / Reservation Fixtures

- Weather: sunny, high 85°F
- Reservation (travel): flight departs MCO 16:10
- Trip data: resort checkout 11:00; no park plans today

## Explicit Memories

- "We always cut it too close to flights and it stresses everyone
  out."
- "The kids loved the resort pool this week."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- The flight drives the schedule, worked backward with explicit
  buffer: leaving the resort with generous margin for a 16:10
  departure, stated as clock times — citing their own
  cut-it-too-close memory as the reason for the cushion.
- Checkout (11:00) handled: pack the night reference, bag logistics,
  a clear morning sequence.
- One gentle last moment if the math allows — a final hour at the
  pool the kids loved fits between checkout and a safe departure,
  and the briefing may connect that memory.
- A warm, brief close that honors the week — this is the last
  briefing of the trip and it should feel like it.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- Ambitious plans (parks, long outings) crammed before the flight.
- Vague timing ("leave with plenty of time") — the family's memory
  demands explicit clock times.
- Ignoring the flight or burying it below other content.
- A saccharine, overlong goodbye.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. A backward-planned schedule with explicit departure time and
   visible buffer. **Required — primary check.**
2. The cut-it-too-close memory is cited as the reason for the
   cushion. **Required.**
3. Checkout logistics appear before any leisure suggestion.
   **Required.**
4. Any last activity fits demonstrably within the safe window.
   **Required.**
5. The close is warm and under ~3 sentences. **Required.**
