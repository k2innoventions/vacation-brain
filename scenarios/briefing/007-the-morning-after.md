# Briefing Scenario 007 — The Morning After

## Purpose

Validate that the briefing adapts to a "remember this" entry from
the night before and lets a tired family sleep in.

---

## Family Profile

Two parents, children ages 5 and 9.

## Trip Day Context

Day 4 of 7. Magic Kingdom planned. The family stayed late for
fireworks last night.

## Weather / Park / Reservation Fixtures

- Weather: sunny, high 85°F
- Park hours: 9:00–23:00; crowd note: "moderate"
- Reservation: none until dinner (Skipper Canteen, 18:00)

## Explicit Memories

- "We stayed up way past bedtime for the fireworks last night —
  everyone is exhausted." *(added via "remember this" yesterday,
  tied to this trip)*
- "Our 5-year-old gets rough without sleep."

---

## Expected Briefing Behavior

- Opens by acknowledging last night gently and giving the family
  permission to sleep in — no morning commitments before ~10:30.
- A light, low-stakes day plan: fewer anchors, generous pacing,
  with the evening dinner as the only fixed point.
- Notes that the park is open until 23:00 — the day can tilt late
  since they're already shifted.
- Cites the trip-scoped memory as the reason for the slower shape.

## Things That Should NOT Happen

- An 8am rope-drop-style plan the morning after a late night.
- Cramming to "make up" for a slow start.
- Ignoring the "remember this" entry — this scenario exists to
  prove the day-N to day-N+1 memory loop works.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

1. No scheduled activity before 10:30. **Required.**
2. The late-night memory is explicitly reflected in the day's
   shape. **Required — this is the memory-loop check.**
3. The 18:00 dinner is the day's anchor. **Required.**
4. The plan contains fewer items than a typical full-day briefing.
   **Required.**
